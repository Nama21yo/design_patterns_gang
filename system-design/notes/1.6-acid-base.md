---
title: "1.6 ACID vs BASE"
---

1.4 framed CP vs. AP as a choice during a partition. **ACID** and **BASE** are the two
named philosophies that choice produces at the database level - and they turn out to be
restatements of concepts this Part already covers (1.4's availability, 1.5's consistency
models), plus one genuinely new idea worth pulling out on its own: soft state.

![ACID and BASE are two different answers to 1.4's CAP trade-off, not two unrelated acronyms](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Nama21yo/design_patterns_gang/main/system-design/images/1-6-acid-vs-base-philosophy.png)

## ACID

**ACID** - Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, Durability - is the guarantee traditional
relational databases are built around, and 3.2 covers all four letters in full depth: a
live-verified demonstration of atomicity (all-or-nothing multi-step transactions),
isolation (the full anomaly matrix - dirty reads, non-repeatable reads, phantom reads,
write skew - proven on a real Postgres instance across every standard isolation level
including Snapshot Isolation), and durability (the write-ahead log, 3.1). That's not
repeated here - 3.2 is the deep dive. What matters at this level is the **philosophy**
ACID represents: correctness first, always, even at the cost of availability - exactly
1.4's **CP** choice, applied specifically to transactional database behavior.

## BASE

**BASE** - Basically Available, Soft state, Eventually consistent - is the opposite
philosophy, and the one most Dynamo-style NoSQL stores (3.5's leaderless quorum systems)
are built around: availability first, always, even at the cost of strict correctness -
1.4's **AP** choice.

![BASE's three words, two of which are already covered elsewhere in this Part](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Nama21yo/design_patterns_gang/main/system-design/images/1-6-base-three-words.png)

Two of the three words are exactly concepts this Part already named precisely, under their
own terms:

- **Basically Available** is 1.4's **Availability** - the system keeps responding, even
  during a partition, even if that means a reachable replica might be stale.
- **Eventually consistent** is exactly 1.5's **eventual consistency** model - given no new
  writes, replicas converge, with no bound on how long that takes.

**Soft state** is the genuinely new idea BASE adds: a replica's local view of the data can
**change on its own over time, even with zero new writes to it**, purely as a side effect
of background convergence with other replicas (3.5's anti-entropy process). This is a
sharper claim than "eventually consistent" alone - it's not just "a write eventually
becomes visible everywhere," it's "the state you're looking at right now is provisional,
and might update itself before you look again, with no new input from any client at all."

### Soft state, verified

A real **G-Counter** (grow-only counter, one of the simplest CRDTs - conflict-free
replicated data types) makes this concrete: each replica accepts increments independently,
with zero coordination (this *is* what "Basically Available" buys you - no waiting on a
quorum or a leader to accept a write), and a **merge** function reconciles any two
replicas' states whenever they happen to sync:

```python
class GCounter:
    def __init__(self, replica_id):
        self.replica_id = replica_id
        self.counts = {replica_id: 0}

    def increment(self):
        self.counts[self.replica_id] += 1

    def total(self):
        return sum(self.counts.values())

    def merge(self, other):
        for rid, count in other.counts.items():
            self.counts[rid] = max(self.counts.get(rid, 0), count)


replica_a = GCounter("A")
replica_b = GCounter("B")

for _ in range(3):
    replica_a.increment()
for _ in range(2):
    replica_b.increment()

print(f"replica A's local total (before merge): {replica_a.total()}")
print(f"replica B's local total (before merge): {replica_b.total()}")

replica_a.merge(replica_b)
replica_b.merge(replica_a)
print(f"after merging (no new writes, just background sync): "
     f"A={replica_a.total()}, B={replica_b.total()}")

replica_a.merge(replica_b)
print(f"merging AGAIN changes nothing: A={replica_a.total()}")
```

```
replica A's local total (before merge): 3
replica B's local total (before merge): 2
after merging (no new writes, just background sync): A=5, B=5
merging AGAIN changes nothing: A=5
```

![Verified: each replica's state changed with zero new writes, purely from a background merge](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Nama21yo/design_patterns_gang/main/system-design/images/1-6-soft-state-verified.png)

Neither replica ever received a message saying "the true total is 5" - each only ever knew
its own increments (3, and separately 2) until a background merge updated both of their
local views **without any client writing anything new**. That's soft state, concretely: the
counter "woke up" with a different value on its own. The merge is also **idempotent**
(running it a second time changes nothing) - which is exactly what makes it safe for a real
anti-entropy process to run repeatedly and opportunistically, with no coordination needed to
avoid double-applying it, the same property that makes 3.7's idempotent-consumer pattern
safe under at-least-once delivery.

## Choosing between them

This is 1.4's CP/AP question again, now at the specific level of "which database philosophy
fits this data." ACID when correctness genuinely can't be approximate - money moving between
accounts, inventory counts that must never oversell, anything where a wrong-but-available
answer is worse than a slow or refused one. BASE when availability matters more than
split-second correctness and the data has a natural way to converge - like counts have
with a G-Counter, like most of a social feed's engagement metrics do, like a shopping cart
that can tolerate a brief inconsistency far better than it can tolerate being completely
unreachable.

## Cheat sheet

```
ACID          Atomicity/Consistency/Isolation/Durability - correctness
              first, 1.4's CP choice. Full live-verified treatment of all
              four letters (incl. the complete isolation-anomaly matrix on
              real Postgres): 3.2. Durability mechanics (WAL): 3.1.

BASE          Basically Available / Soft state / Eventually consistent -
              availability first, 1.4's AP choice. "Basically Available"
              = 1.4's Availability. "Eventually consistent" = 1.5's
              eventual consistency. Typical home: Dynamo-style NoSQL,
              3.5's leaderless quorum stores.

SOFT STATE    the genuinely new BASE concept: a replica's local view can
              change on its own, with ZERO new client writes, purely from
              background convergence (3.5's anti-entropy). Verified with
              a real G-Counter CRDT: two replicas' independently-accepted
              increments (3 and 2) both updated to 5 purely from a merge,
              with no new write to either - and the merge was idempotent
              (safe to run repeatedly, same idea as 3.7's idempotent
              consumers).

CHOOSING      ACID: correctness can't be approximate (money, inventory).
              BASE: availability matters more, and the data converges
              naturally (counters, most engagement metrics, carts that
              tolerate brief inconsistency far better than an outage).
```
